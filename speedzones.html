What this tests—and what it doesn't

The commutator reveals spatial variation in a phase-delay map. A uniformly delayed field produces exactly zero wake under periodic boundary conditions; a localized delay changes neighboring phases and gives a nonzero response. Pure phase multiplication and periodic shifts preserve energy, whereas attenuating transport loses it.

Those are mathematical controls, not trained vision-model wins. Directly reading the phase map or its finite differences may solve a task just as well. The Python v2 benchmark makes those comparisons on held-out synthetic scenes, including an equally sized amplitude-coded baseline.

A true eikonal arrival-time gradient is not necessarily a persistent physical wake after a pulse disappears. We simulate a phase mask under prescribed discrete operations, not refracted rays, membrane electrophysiology or lipid biochemistry.
