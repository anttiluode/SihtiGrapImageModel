The gate result

Question-dependent access helps. Operator-order advantage remains unproven.

The question-directed path sees the relevant region. Fixed paths see all regions regardless of the question, and fixed passive ports see only two untransported sites. At four measurements, targeted wave reads average 93.58%, versus 65.31% for question-independent routes. However, four direct local phase samples average 99.02%.

The complex differences of the two phase/shift orders perform relatively poorly. The raw paired order measurements can be much more informative; particularly, with two shots and frozen seed 97, paired orders reach 98.73%. This effect is not matched by seed 41, and reading phase directly still wins.

New: common-path phase cancellation

The exact endpoint ratio is real, but it is not universally a better reader. For a matched 12-step path, measuring phase→shift and shift→phase yields z1×conj(z2) = exp(i×(phase(source) − phase(port))). Intermediate contributions cancel algebraically. The new v3b frozen-checkpoint test shows 98.58% with two readings on seed 97, but only 59.23% on seed 41. Its identical source-site direct control scores 99.95% and 62.74%, respectively; the chosen source matters. The raw paired response scores 98.73% and 78.37%: shared-path phase can also contain useful information rather than only nuisance. Explore the new controlled ratio experiment →

A single active shot traverses 12 phase cells and returns one complex detector value. A direct phase shot samples one phase cell and reads it directly. Thus the experiment matches reported measurement count, not underlying phase-cell accesses or compute costs. No feedback-based route adaptation, PDE wavefront, physical speed map, or motion/occlusion history is used.
